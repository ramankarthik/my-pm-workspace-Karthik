# change_log.md

| Date | Change | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| 2026-09-09 | Day 1 of discovery phase | Investigating Day-7 retention drop (48% → 39%) following the v2 streak redesign; kicked off with problem framing from the team Slack thread. |
| 2026-09-09 | Fixed "Not now" on the Comeback screen prototype: now exits to a home-screen stand-in with the streak-freeze offer's 48-hour availability window, instead of silently routing into the 60-second lesson. | Usability testing surfaced this as a functional bug — pressing "Not now" led to the lesson anyway, contradicting the button's own label — and a real open question ("will this option still be here later, and for how long?") that the screen didn't answer. Chosen as the single highest-priority fix over addressing entry-point discoverability (unclear how users arrive at this screen), since it was a concrete, in-scope defect on this screen rather than a broader flow/navigation question requiring a home-screen design that doesn't exist yet. |
| 2026-09-09 | Added explicit "Free, always — no catch, no subscription" reassurance to the lock-card copy (Comeback screen) and "and it's free" to the freeze-unlock screen. | **Source: simulated persona role-play (Claude playing Priya, Tom, and Amara), not a real usability session — treat as a lower-confidence, directional signal, not equivalent to the earlier real usability round.** Role-play surfaced Tom — the persona closest to the real churned user this feature targets — reading the lesson-gated streak-freeze as a possible paywall/manipulation tactic ("is this going to turn into a premium feature the moment I tap it?"), given his backstory of being burned by habit apps before. This was the most severe risk surfaced (threatens the core hypothesis with the exact audience the fix is meant to win back), and a low-cost copy fix, so it was prioritized over Priya's request for earlier/proactive reassurance and Amara's confusion at the prototype's hardcoded streak numbers not matching her own persona state (a mock-data limitation, not a design flaw, and out of scope for a copy-level fix). Worth re-validating this specific concern with real users in the next actual usability round. |
