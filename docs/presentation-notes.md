# Speaker Notes: Streakly Comeback Experience

Full sentences, spoken form. Pairs with [docs/presentation.md](presentation.md).

---

## Slide 1: The Problem

Day-7 retention dropped from 48% to 39% after the v2 streak redesign shipped — that's the number driving everything on this slide and everything after it. Across every research source we have — interviews, feedback, competitive research — the same pattern keeps showing up: when a user breaks a streak, the app just resets to zero and says nothing, and that silence is the single clearest driver of this drop. I want you to leave this slide with one thing: this isn't a vague retention problem, it's a specific, well-understood moment in the product where we're losing people. That's what the rest of this deck is about fixing.

## Slide 2: Why Now

Since we last talked about this, we didn't just keep researching — we ran a real pilot, testing a personalized comeback experience against a control group. What we learned is genuinely encouraging: the treatment group showed a 30-point lift in Day-7 retention and a 14-point lift in Day-30, and that lift held up across multiple touchpoints rather than fading after one send. I also want to flag something on purpose: we caught a real data-quality issue in how the two test groups were assigned, and I'm bringing that to you rather than hiding it, because I want you to trust the number I eventually give you. The reason this is urgent now is simple — every week we don't act, another cohort of new users lands at the declining baseline instead of getting the experience that produced this lift.

## Slide 3: The Proposal

What we're proposing is a personalized comeback experience: when someone breaks a streak, instead of a silent reset, they see their progress acknowledged and a real way back in. I want to be equally clear about what this is not — it is not a fix for notification tone or frequency, which is a real issue but a separate workstream we're tracking on its own, so we don't want this deck to get pulled into that conversation. And it is not a proposal to scale based on the raw pilot number as it stands today — I'm going to show you why in a minute, and what we want to do about it before we commit to a specific figure.

## Slide 4: Evidence

Here's the data behind the recommendation: 76% Day-7 retention in the treatment group versus 46% in control, and 36% versus 22% at Day-30, with engagement climbing from 28% to 56% across the test while control stayed flat the whole time. But I don't want this to just be a numbers slide — this matches exactly what we heard directly from users before we ever built anything. Tom told us, in his own words, that when he lost his streak there was no way back and he simply gave up, and that a competitor's streak freeze is what felt forgiving enough to switch to. Priya's experience shows the flip side — acknowledgment and celebration of progress is literally what made the habit stick for her. And this isn't just a concept slide — we have a working, tested prototype behind these numbers, not just a plan on paper.

## Slide 5: The Plan

I want to be straightforward with you: we don't have a full rollout timeline locked yet, and I'd rather tell you that directly than paper over it. What we do have is a clear next set of steps — first, we correct the pilot analysis for the data-quality issue I flagged a minute ago, then we hit a decision checkpoint to confirm the real effect size, and only then do we move to a scaled rollout with a timeline we set together, today, as part of this conversation. The two risks I want you holding in your head are these: if we present the raw pilot number without correcting it, we risk committing to a figure that doesn't hold up at scale: and if we wait too long to decide, we keep losing retention every single week in the meantime.

## Slide 6: The Ask

So here's what I actually need from you: approval to move into a larger, corrected rollout of this comeback experience, and a decision on timeline so we can slot the corrected analysis and the scale-up directly into the sprint plan. I'm not asking you to sign off on the 76% number today — I'm asking you to greenlight the path that gets us a number we can both stand behind. If you need more detail on any of this after today, I'm glad to follow up async, but the one decision I need before we leave this room is whether we move forward and by when.
