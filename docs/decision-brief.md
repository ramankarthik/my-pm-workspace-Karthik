# Decision Brief: Recovering Day-7 Retention

**To:** Marcus, Head of Product
**From:** Engagement squad

*Note: this brief draws on three research sources — user interviews, NPS analysis, and the competitive matrix. A fourth requested source (`02-research/competitive-reddit.md`, Reddit sentiment on competitors) was not available in the workspace and is excluded here.*

## Situation

Day-7 retention has dropped from 48% to 39% following the v2 streak redesign, concentrated in users who break their streak in week 1 and don't return. Across interviews, NPS feedback, and competitive research, the pattern is consistent: the app's response to a broken streak — a silent reset with no acknowledgment or way back — is the clearest driver of this churn.

## Key Findings

- **Breaking a streak reads as final, not recoverable.** The top NPS theme (3 of 10 verbatims) and the strongest interview evidence (Tom R.'s churn story) both center on the same moment: streak resets to zero, no acknowledgment, no way back.
- **Users want a coach, not a scorekeeper.** Tied for the top NPS theme: users explicitly want the app to acknowledge their progress rather than punish a miss — echoed by Priya's interview, where celebration of a 30-day streak is what made the habit stick.
- **Competitors don't own this either — it's genuine white space.** Duolingo offers a forgiveness mechanic (Streak Freeze / streak repair), but it's a paid, transactional purchase, not a personalized comeback — Babbel, Elevate, and Headway show no comparable comeback flow at all.
- **A secondary, related issue: notification fatigue.** 2 of 10 NPS verbatims describe reminders as nagging or random, severe enough that users disable notifications entirely — compounding the punishing-reset experience rather than causing it independently. Recommend tracking as its own workstream, not folding into the comeback fix.
- **An unresolved, single-source signal worth watching:** one interview (Amara L., 4 days in) shows streak anxiety *before* any break occurs, suggesting the all-or-nothing mechanic may create pressure from day one — not corroborated elsewhere yet, and no competitor addresses this proactively either.

## Options Considered

1. **Do nothing / monitor further** — risk: the churn pattern is consistent across every source we have; further delay likely compounds the retention loss.
2. **Fix notifications only** — addresses a real but secondary complaint; unlikely to move Day-7 retention on its own since the reset/acknowledgment issue is the higher-frequency, higher-severity driver.
3. **Build a personalized comeback experience** (streak-freeze + acknowledgment of prior progress, replacing the silent reset) — directly targets the highest-frequency, multi-source-corroborated driver, and occupies white space no competitor currently owns.

## Recommended Action

Greenlight discovery-to-prototype work on a personalized comeback experience (progress acknowledgment + forgiveness mechanic) as the primary fix for Day-7 retention, with notification cadence tracked as a separate, secondary workstream.

## Why Now

Three independent sources — interviews, NPS feedback, and competitive research — converge on the same root cause, giving unusually high confidence for a discovery-stage hypothesis; the longer the current reset experience stays live, the more week-1 users are lost to a fixable, well-understood gap that no competitor has closed either.
