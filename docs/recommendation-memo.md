# Comeback Experience: Results Memo

**To:** Marcus, Head of Product
**From:** Engagement squad

## Situation

We tested a redesigned weekly comeback experience (`summary_v1`) against control for week-5 users, to address the Day-7 retention decline we've tracked across cohorts (60% → 44% from weeks 1-4).

## Evidence

- **Day-7 retention: 76% (treatment) vs. 46% (control), +30 points. Day-30: 36% vs. 22%, +14 points.**
- **The lift is sustained, not a novelty spike:** open rate climbed from 28% to 56% across the 4 sends for treatment, while control stayed flat at 4-6% the entire time — and action-taken rate was consistently higher every week (12-22% vs. 0-4%).
- **One data-quality flag before this number goes further:** the two test groups weren't fully balanced going in — treatment users were twice as likely to have already set a goal before the test started (44% vs. 22%), and goal-setting is the strongest single predictor of retention we've found in the whole dataset. Some of the 30-point gap is very likely this pre-existing difference, not the emails themselves.

## Recommendation

Scale the comeback experience beyond week 5, but rerun the Day-7 number controlling for the goal-setting imbalance before we present a retention-lift figure externally or to the board.

## Ask

Approval to move into a larger, properly balanced rollout — and a decision on timeline so we can slot the corrected re-analysis and scale-up into the sprint plan.

## Risk If We Wait

Every week we don't act, new cohorts keep landing at the declining ~44-48% baseline instead of the ~76% we saw in treatment, and we still won't have a clean, defensible effect-size number until the imbalance is corrected.
