# Interview Synthesis

Source: 3 user interviews — Priya S. (power user, 14-month streak), Tom R. (churned, broke a 12-day streak at 5 weeks), Amara L. (new user, 4 days in).

## Top 5 Themes

### 1. Losing a streak feels like losing everything you built
- Tom: "Then I traveled for work, missed two days, and came back to a big fat zero."
- Amara: "I don't want to lose everything I've built after four days."

### 2. The "you lost your streak" moment is harsh and offers no way back
- Tom: "The app sent me this 'you lost your streak' notification that just made me feel bad."
- Tom: "There was no way to recover it, nothing. So I gave up."

### 3. Competitors already offer a forgiveness mechanic Streakly lacks
- Tom: "I switched to Duolingo, at least there a missed day doesn't wipe everything, and a streak freeze feels forgiving."
- *(Only one interviewee raised this directly — flagging as single-source, not corroborated across users.)*

### 4. Celebrating progress is what makes the habit stick
- Priya: "The thing that made it stick was hitting a 30-day streak and the app actually celebrating it, that little moment of 'look what you built.'"
- Priya: "After that I did not want to lose it."

### 5. Streak anxiety shows up before any streak is ever broken
- Amara: "I'm already stressed about the streak, I keep thinking, what happens if I miss a day?"
- Amara: "the pressure is starting to feel like a chore instead of a game."

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **The streak mechanic cuts both ways.** For Priya, the all-or-nothing streak plus celebration is exactly what made the habit stick — but she notes it took ~3 weeks to get there and believes "most people quit long before the habit forms." Tom and Amara are evidence of that earlier drop-off: the same mechanic that retains Priya appears to be actively pushing Tom and Amara out before the habit has time to form.
- **Content vs. mechanic.** Amara describes the lessons themselves as "genuinely fun," but says the streak pressure is turning the experience into "a chore instead of a game" — the core product (micro-learning) is working, but the streak wrapper around it may be undermining it for new users.

## Single Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

Amara — only 4 days in and nowhere near breaking her streak — is *already* anxious about losing it. This suggests the week-1 retention risk isn't only about what happens after a break (the "comeback" moment), but the anticipatory pressure the all-or-nothing streak mechanic creates from day one. A forgiveness mechanic (like the streak freeze Tom wished he'd had) may need to be visible and available early — not only offered as recovery after a break — to reduce the chore-like pressure Amara describes before it drives her to quit.
