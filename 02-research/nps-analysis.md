# NPS Feedback Analysis

Source: 10 raw NPS verbatims, Streakly users.

## Themes Mentioned More Than Once (Ranked by Frequency)

| Rank | Theme | Mentions | Verbatims |
|------|-------|----------|-----------|
| 1 (tie) | Breaking a streak leads to disengagement/churn — losing it removes the reason to keep using the app | 3 | "Felt pointless to start over." / "The moment I lost my streak the whole thing lost its meaning." / "The streak is the only thing keeping me engaged, but the second I lost it, I was done." |
| 1 (tie) | App feels like it punishes/scorekeeps rather than acknowledging progress or coaching users back | 3 | "I want Streakly to feel like a coach... not a scorekeeper that punishes me." / "Nothing acknowledges where I am." / "...instead of making me feel like I failed and have to start from scratch." |
| 3 (tie) | Users want a recovery/forgiveness mechanic (streak freeze) that doesn't exist today | 2 | "Other apps let you freeze a streak. Why not this one?" / "I wish it would make coming back easier..." |
| 3 (tie) | Notifications feel like nagging — frequency and relevance are off | 2 | "...the daily reminder just started to feel like nagging." / "I got three in one afternoon and just turned them all off." |

*Note: one verbatim ("I wish it would make coming back easier instead of making me feel like I failed...") touches both the recovery-mechanic theme and the punishment/acknowledgment theme — counted in both.*

## Praise vs. Complaints

**Praise**
- "The first week was genuinely fun."
- "Love the lessons."

**Complaints**
- Streak reset to zero after a single missed day, with no way to recover it.
- No acknowledgment of progress or where the user is (2-day streak vs. returning after 2 weeks — same static home screen).
- App feels punitive ("scorekeeper") rather than supportive ("coach").
- Notifications are frequent, random-feeling, and read as nagging rather than helpful.
- Users forget the app exists between sessions and get no useful pull back in.

*(Single-mention complaint, not counted in the ranked table above: one user said they forget the app exists after a couple of days and would come back "if it pulled me back with something useful.")*

## Top 3 Actionable Issues

1. **No recovery mechanic after a broken streak.** The single most-cited reason for total disengagement is that breaking a streak feels final — no freeze, no way back, nothing acknowledging progress made. This is the most direct, highest-frequency fix candidate (touches 5 of 10 verbatims across the top two themes).
2. **The app doesn't acknowledge user state.** The home screen shows the same thing whether a user is on day 2 or returning after weeks away — users explicitly want the app to recognize where they are rather than reset silently to zero.
3. **Notification cadence/relevance is off.** Multiple users describe reminders as nagging or random, to the point of disabling them entirely — a fatigue problem independent of the streak-reset issue, but compounding it (a punishing reset followed by a nagging notification is a bad sequence).

## For Marcus

This NPS sample reinforces the Slack-thread hypothesis: the post-break experience (silent reset, no acknowledgment, no recovery) is the clearest, most-repeated driver of disengagement in this feedback. It also surfaces a second, related issue — notification fatigue — that's frequent enough to treat as its own workstream rather than folding it entirely into the "comeback experience" scope. Recommend we scope the streak-freeze / comeback screen as the primary fix, and track notification cadence as a secondary item worth its own review.
